# Spotify's 1,000 Stream Rule: Why Your Small Tracks Earn Nothing

**The Pulse · The RAAYDR Blog**
*By Ric, Founder of RAAYDR · October 2026*

If a track of yours had fewer than 1,000 streams on Spotify in the last twelve months, it earned you nothing. Not a small amount. Nothing. That has been the rule since April 2024, and most artists only find out when they go looking for money that was never there.

Here is how it works, where that money actually goes, and why it is now being challenged in court.

## What is the 1,000 stream rule?

Spotify announced it in November 2023 and switched it on for every artist in April 2024. A track has to reach at least 1,000 streams in the previous twelve months before it earns any recorded music royalties. It also has to reach a minimum number of unique listeners, which Spotify does not publish, so you cannot get over the line by playing your own song on repeat.

The rule works track by track. One song in your catalogue can qualify while the one next to it does not, and a track can drop out again if its streams fall away.

## Where does the money go?

It does not disappear and it does not go back to you. Spotify's own figure is about $40 million a year, and that money is moved to the tracks that did pass the threshold. The pool stays the same size. It just stops including you.

Spotify's argument is that these tiny payments often never reached artists anyway, because distributors set minimum withdrawals and banks charge fees on small transfers. It also says 99.5% of all streams are of tracks above the line.

Look at that the other way round. The 0.5% of streams below the line are mostly the newest and smallest artists on the platform, and their share is handed to the biggest.

## Is this the same as a payout threshold?

No, and the difference matters. A payout threshold means your earnings wait until they are big enough to send. Under the 1,000 stream rule the earnings are never created for you in the first place. A track that does 900 streams this year does not carry them into next year's total as money owed.

## Who is challenging it?

In June 2026, Connecticut musician and lawyer Mark Kratter sued Spotify, asking the court to find the threshold an unfair and deceptive practice under state law. He also claims a March 2026 change in how Spotify filters plays stopped legitimate streams of his music being counted. The case is in the US District Court for the District of Connecticut and nothing has been decided. Whatever happens, the rule is in force today.

## What else changed at the same time?

Two more rules arrived alongside it. Labels and distributors are charged per track when Spotify finds flagrant artificial streaming on their content. And functional audio, such as white noise and rain sounds, now has to play for two minutes rather than thirty seconds before it counts as a stream.

## What can you do about it?

1. **Check your catalogue.** In Spotify for Artists, look at each track's streams over the last twelve months. Anything under 1,000 is earning nothing right now.
2. **Do not buy streams to get over the line.** Spotify removes artificial plays and your distributor can be charged for them. We explain why in [Can You Buy Spotify Streams?](/pulse/can-you-buy-spotify-streams)
3. **Release less, push harder.** Ten singles at 400 streams each earn nothing. Two at 2,000 each both qualify.
4. **Point your fans somewhere that pays for them.** The real problem sits underneath the threshold: your fans' money goes into one big pool and is shared out by everyone's streams, not theirs. ## FAQ

**Does Spotify pay for tracks under 1,000 streams?**
No. Since April 2024, a track needs at least 1,000 streams in the previous twelve months, plus a minimum number of unique listeners, to earn recorded music royalties on Spotify.

**Does the 1,000 stream rule affect songwriting royalties?**
Spotify has said the threshold applies to recorded music royalties. Publishing royalties, which songwriters collect through societies such as PRS, are calculated separately.

**Where does the money from tracks under 1,000 streams go?**
Spotify says it is moved to tracks above the threshold. It puts the total at about $40 million a year.

**Is the 1,000 streams per track or per artist?**
Per track. One song can qualify while another by the same artist does not.

**Can I play my own track to reach 1,000 streams?**
No. Spotify also requires a minimum number of unique listeners, and artificial plays are removed.
