# Spotify Wrapped Shows Your Top Artist. Did Your Subscription Actually Pay Them?

**The Pulse · The RAAYDR Blog**
*By Ric, Founder of RAAYDR · November 2026*

**Probably not much of it.** Spotify pays artists by their share of every stream on the platform, so your subscription goes into one big pot first. Your top artist gets a slice of the pot based on everyone's listening, and your own listening barely moves it.

Wrapped season is about to land. Spotify hasn't confirmed the date for 2026, but going by the last few years it will be the start of December, and your feed will fill up with top fives and listening minutes and that slightly embarrassing song you had on repeat in March.

It's a lovely bit of marketing. We're not here to ruin it. We just think there's one slide missing.

## What does Spotify Wrapped actually count?

Your listening from January to roughly mid November. Top artists, top songs, genres, minutes. Spotify uses the last couple of weeks of the year to check the data and build the stories you share.

What it doesn't show you is money. There's no slide that says "you paid £155.88 this year, and here's who got it." And there's a reason for that, which is that Spotify couldn't really make that slide even if it wanted to. Your money doesn't travel to the artists you played. It travels to a pool.

## Where does your {{spotify.subscriptionPrice}} a month go?

Spotify Premium Individual is {{spotify.subscriptionPrice}} a month in the UK. Once the platform takes its share, the money left for music gets paid out by what Spotify calls streamshare. In its own words, if an artist's catalogue makes up 1% of total streams, it gets 1% of total royalties.

Total streams. Across every listener, in every country, on every plan.

So picture it like a jar on a bar. Everyone's subscription goes in. At the end of the month the jar gets split by who got played the most overall. You might have spent all year with one artist nobody else has heard of, and they get paid as if your year never happened, because in a jar that size it basically didn't.

## A simple example: three listeners, one jar

This is a stripped down illustration with made up round numbers, ignoring the platform's cut and the publishing deductions, so you can see the mechanism and nothing else.

Three people each pay £10 a month. That's £30 in the jar.

**Amy** plays one independent artist all month. 100 plays.

**Ben** plays a huge global act. 1,000 plays.

**Cal** has the same huge act on all day. 1,900 plays.

That's 3,000 plays in total.

| | Pooled (pay by streamshare) | Follow each listener's money |
|---|---|---|
| Independent artist (Amy's only artist) | 100 ÷ 3,000 × £30 = **£1** | All of Amy's £10 = **£10** |
| Huge global act | 2,900 ÷ 3,000 × £30 = **£29** | Ben's £10 + Cal's £10 = **£20** |

Amy paid £10 and her artist got £1. The other £9 of her money went to an artist she never played once, because Cal leaves his music on while he sleeps.

Scale that up to hundreds of millions of subscribers and you get the thing independent artists keep telling us. Thousands of listeners. A royalty statement that looks like a typo.

## Is the pooled model illegal or a scam?

No. It's how the major services have paid out for years, and it's simple to run at enormous scale. It also isn't a secret, Spotify explains streamshare openly in its annual Loud & Clear report.

It's just a choice. And it's a choice that quietly favours whoever gets the most total plays, which tends to be the biggest acts and, increasingly, whoever is good at gaming play counts. We wrote about that side of it in [Can You Buy Spotify Streams?](/pulse/can-you-buy-spotify-streams).

## What would "follow your money" look like instead?

That's the right hand column in the table. Each listener's subscription gets split only between the artists that listener actually played, in proportion to how much they played them. If you only ever listened to one artist this month, that artist gets the whole artist share of your subscription. If you split your time between five, they share it.

The industry calls this user centric payment. We built RAAYDR on it, and then went a step further and measure attention (actual time spent listening) rather than raw play counts, so leaving one track on loop doesn't outweigh someone who sat with a whole record. The full mechanics are in [What Is Attention Based Streaming Payment?](/pulse/what-is-attention-based-streaming-payment), and the exact split of a subscription, line by line, is in [Where Your £9.99 Actually Goes on RAAYDR](/pulse/where-your-9-99-actually-goes).

The short version. Your money follows your ears. And your favourite artist knows you're there.

## So should you stop caring about your Wrapped?

Course not. Share it, argue about it, be smug about your top genre. It's fun.

But maybe when you see your top artist come up this year, think about Amy for a second. If that artist is someone small, someone you found through a mate or a DJ or a random late night rabbit hole, there's a decent chance your year of listening paid them less than the price of a coffee. We think that's worth knowing, even if the slide for it doesn't exist yet.

## FAQ

**Does Spotify pay artists based on my own listening?**
No. Spotify pays by streamshare, meaning each artist's share of total streams across the whole platform. Your listening counts as part of that total, but your subscription isn't sent to the specific artists you played.

**What is the difference between pro rata and user centric streaming payments?**
Pro rata (pooled) payment puts every subscription into one pot and splits it by total plays across all listeners. User centric payment splits each listener's subscription only between the artists that listener played. RAAYDR uses user centric payment based on listening time.

**When does Spotify Wrapped 2026 come out?**
Spotify hasn't announced it. Recent editions arrived on a Wednesday in late November or early December, so the first week of December 2026 is the likely window.
